The basics
10 golden rules of safe renting
Follow these rules every time, without exception — no matter how urgent or good a deal seems.
1
Never pay before viewing
This is the single most important rule. No legitimate landlord will ask for a deposit, first month's rent, or any payment before you have physically visited the property and met them in person.
2
Always get a written contract
Under the Danish Rent Act (Lejeloven), you are legally entitled to a written rental contract. Never move in or pay a deposit without one. A verbal agreement offers you almost no legal protection.
3
Keep communication on BoligMatch
Be cautious if an owner immediately pushes you to WhatsApp or email before you've had any meaningful conversation. Keeping messages on-platform means there is a record if something goes wrong.
4
Never pay with gift cards or crypto
No legitimate landlord in Denmark will ask for payment via gift cards (Google Play, Amazon, iTunes), cryptocurrency, or any payment method that is irreversible and untraceable.
5
Be suspicious of prices far below market
A 20m² room in central Copenhagen for DKK 2,000/month does not exist. Scammers use unrealistically low prices to attract desperate students. If it seems too good to be true, it is.
6
Never pay to view a property
Viewing a rental property is always free. If an owner asks you to pay a "viewing fee", a "holding deposit", or an "administration fee" before you have seen the room — this is a scam.
7
Verify the landlord's identity
Ask to see a utility bill or official document proving the owner lives at or owns the property. A legitimate landlord will be happy to provide this. Be wary of anyone who refuses or makes excuses.
8
Do a reverse image search on photos
Scammers steal photos from other websites. Right-click any listing photo and choose "Search image". If the same photo appears on multiple different listings or real estate sites with different addresses, it is fake.
9
Be wary of "I'm abroad" excuses
A very common scam involves a landlord claiming they are abroad for work, a military deployment, or a family emergency — and therefore cannot show you the property or meet in person. This is a classic red flag.
10
Report anything suspicious
If a listing or message feels wrong — even if you can't explain why — use the 🚩 Report button. You may save another student from being scammed. Reports are reviewed by admins within 24 hours.
Warning signs
Rental scam red flags
If you see any of these in a listing or conversation, stop immediately and report the listing.
💸
Unusually low price
Rent 40–60% below similar listings in the same area. Scammers price low to attract many victims quickly.
✈️
"I'm currently abroad"
Owner claims to be in another country for work, military deployment, or missionary work and can only send the keys by mail after payment.
⚡
Pressure to decide fast
"Other students are interested — pay the deposit today to secure it." Urgency is a manipulation tactic to prevent you thinking clearly.
💳
Unusual payment method
Requests for Western Union, MoneyGram, Zelle, gift cards, or cryptocurrency. These are untraceable and scammers love them.
📷
Stock or stolen photos
Perfect magazine-quality photos, or photos that appear on multiple listings with different addresses when reverse-searched.
📱
Immediate push to WhatsApp
Asking you to move the conversation off-platform immediately, before any meaningful exchange, to avoid leaving a record.
🔑
"Keys will be mailed to you"
After paying, the owner claims they will post the keys to you. You pay, receive nothing, and the owner disappears.
📄
No contract offered
Owner says a written contract "isn't necessary" or will "come later." You have no legal protection without one under Danish law.
🌐
Broken or template English
Messages that read like automated translations, or responses that don't quite answer your specific questions and seem copy-pasted.
Know your enemy
Common rental scam types
Understanding how scams work is the best defence against them.
👻
The Phantom Rental
A scammer creates a listing for a property they do not own or that does not exist. They may steal real photos from legitimate listings on other platforms. After you pay a deposit, they disappear. The property may exist but they have no connection to it.
✓ Always verify the owner's connection to the property before paying anything.
🔑
The "Keys by Mail" Scam
The owner says they are abroad (missionary work, military, oil rig) and will mail you the keys after you pay the deposit. They may send fake "proof of ownership" documents. You pay, they send nothing, and they disappear. This is one of the most reported scams in European student housing.
✓ If you cannot view in person, the answer is simply: don't pay.
👯
The Multiple Victims Scam
A scammer lists one property and collects deposits from multiple students simultaneously. Each victim thinks they have secured the room. On move-in day, multiple people arrive at the same address — none of them have a valid lease. The scammer has disappeared with all the deposits.
✓ A signed lease in your name protects you. If no contract is offered, walk away.
💰
The Excessive Deposit Demand
Not technically a scam, but illegal. Under Danish Rent Act (Lejeloven), the maximum combined deposit and prepaid rent is 3 months rent deposit + 3 months prepaid rent = 6 months total. Anyone demanding more is breaking Danish law.
✓ Know your rights: max 3 months deposit. Report excessive demands to the rent tribunal (Huslejenævnet).
Money matters
Safe vs. unsafe payment methods
Always pay in a way that creates a paper trail and can be reversed if fraud occurs.
✓ Safe — use these
✓ Bank transfer (wire) to a Danish bank account — creates a paper trail. Always note "deposit for [address]" in the reference.
✓ MobilePay (Danish bank app) — common in Denmark, traceable, and reversible in fraud cases if reported quickly.
✓ In-person cash — only after signing the contract and receiving a dated, signed receipt. Keep the receipt.
✓ Credit card where accepted — offers chargeback rights if the transaction is fraudulent.
✗ Unsafe — never use these
✗ Western Union / MoneyGram — cash-based wire services with no fraud protection. Money cannot be recovered.
✗ Gift cards (Google Play, Amazon, iTunes) — a landlord asking for gift card numbers is 100% a scam, without exception.
✗ Cryptocurrency — irreversible and untraceable. No legitimate landlord in Denmark will request this.
✗ PayPal Friends & Family — offers no buyer protection. Scammers specifically request this to prevent chargebacks.
✗ Cash without a receipt — if you pay cash, insist on a signed, dated, itemised receipt every single time.

Official resources
If you have been scammed, or want to know your rights, these are the official places to go.
🚔
Danish Police — Report Fraud
Report rental fraud or financial scams directly to the Danish police online. File a report even if you are not sure it is a crime.
politi.dk ↗
📜
Danish Rent Act — Lejeloven
Official consolidated text of the Danish Rent Act in English. Know the legal maximum deposit, your rights to a contract, and tenant protections.
lejeloven.dk ↗
🏛️
International House Copenhagen
Guidance for international arrivals including CPR registration, housing rights, and official support for students new to Denmark.
ihcph.kk.dk ↗
🎓
Study in Denmark — Housing Guide
Official guidance from the Danish Agency for Higher Education on finding housing as an international student in Denmark.
studyindenmark.dk ↗
📊
ESU & ESN Housing Report 2023
Research report surveying 9,000+ international students on housing challenges, scam experiences, and deposit issues across Europe.
esu-online.org ↗

Huslejenævnet — Rent Tribunal
Denmark's official rent tribunal. If a landlord has charged an illegal deposit or broken your tenant rights, you can file a formal complaint here.
